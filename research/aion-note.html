Research note / AION

Exploring self-developing systems.

A research perspective on persistence, memory, evaluation and the gap between ambitious goals and demonstrated capabilities.

October 2026 / Founder's research direction

Most AI applications are built around individual tasks: a user asks, a model answers, and a workflow ends. More capable agents can use tools, delegate steps or store memory. That is useful, but it does not by itself establish a continuously developing artificial being.

Cognitive OS / AION asks a different question: how might one persistent artificial system learn from experience, preserve useful discoveries, recover from failures and improve not only its abilities but also the machinery that makes improvement possible?

Ambition and evidence are different things

A code change is not automatically an intelligence improvement. A single higher benchmark score is not evidence of lasting development. Parallel experiments are meaningful only if they are evaluated, understood and incorporated into later behavior in a verifiable way.

The existing project contains experimental implementation and verification work. It does not claim that AGI, ASI, or a sustained increase in general intelligence has been demonstrated.

Open research questions

Can useful knowledge survive interruptions? Can an improved capability transfer to unfamiliar tasks? Can the system identify flaws in its own learning and testing methods? Can failures be converted into durable experience instead of being discarded? These remain subjects for investigation, not established results.

Why share this direction?

Meaningful research needs criticism, reproducibility, resources and collaborators. We are beginning to explain the broad goals of Cognitive OS publicly while keeping private architecture and code confidential.

Our aim is to test ideas rigorously, report limits plainly and learn from experiments — including experiments that fail.
